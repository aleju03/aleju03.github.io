# src/game — CLAUDE.md

Guidance that only applies inside the game runtime, so it loads only when you work here. The repo-wide rules (the layer cake, the collision contract, the far-plane rule, the React-free/renderer-optional rule) stay in the root `CLAUDE.md` and are still in context. `src/game/README.md` is the long-form map and how-to-add-things guide; this file is the short list of things that will bite you.

## The two subsystems this file covers

- `src/game/world/` — **the open world**: an endless, chunk-streamed procedural planet with Earth-like biomes, water and walkable cities. Everything is a pure function of (x, z) — hashed by position, never streamed — so a chunk rebuilds identically whichever direction you arrive from. `houseWorld.ts` still owns the property line inward and the generator treats that rectangle as a hole (`grid.ts`'s `RESERVED`); the world grid is offset so chunk (0,0) is the house's block and the generated street lands exactly where the old hand-made one was. The three numbers that place the house on the planet (`land.ts`'s `WORLD_X/Z` and `CLIMATE_X/Z`) were calibrated by probing the fields, not guessed — move them and re-probe rather than hunting for a kind seed. `README.md`'s "The open world" section is the guide. It also owns what *moves* out there but isn't part of the ground: `birds.ts`'s flocks are session state like the fleet — one instanced draw, the flap in the vertex shader, rings re-cut beyond the fog when the walker outruns them, built and ticked from `outsideWorld.ts` because that seam is where the sky's daylight and the world's terrain height are both in hand.

- `src/game/vehicles/` — **the fleet**: a car, a boat and a helicopter, one per medium, so the sea and the mountains are somewhere you can actually get to. Models are procedural like everything else — `parts.ts` lofts cross-sections (`ringSuper`'s exponent covers an ellipse, a rounded rectangle and a concave V with one number), and `materials.ts` gives them clearcoat paint reflecting a *painted* equirect env map repainted off the sky's own numbers, because there is no environment to capture. Each machine owns its dynamics; `registry.ts` owns the fleet, the collision bookkeeping, enter/exit and a **fixed 1/120 s substep** (50 ms of explicit Euler through a wheel spring is not a suspension). Three rules bite: a vehicle must never see its own collision box (`supportY` has no owner concept, so it would climb itself — the registry empties the box before each tick); what a walker collides with is each machine's oriented `hull`, its box being only the broad phase around that (the AABB of a car parked askew is mostly invisible wall, and the helicopter's was an 18-unit rectangle around a cabin with a stick out the back); and the fleet is **session state, not world state** — nothing is written into the world, and a reload puts all three back on their probed home spots. `README.md`'s "The fleet" section is the guide.

## Conventions that matter in here

- **A vaultable obstacle needs headroom in time, not just in height.** A box stops blocking the instant the feet clear its top, so what matters is how long the jump arc spends above it versus how deep the box is. The hop apex is ~2.08 units; the yard fence looked unjumpable at a 1.9-high, 0.8-deep box (a 0.05 s window, two handspans of travel) and works at 1.7 by 0.44. Check both numbers when adding something meant to be climbed over.
- **Colour jitter must be multiplicative.** `Color.getHSL` reports lightness in the renderer's *linear* working space, not sRGB, so a tree trunk sits at l ≈ 0.04 and an `offsetHSL(_, _, -0.07)` clamps it to pure black. That bug blacked out half of every forest. Scale channels instead.
- **A swept limb needs a parallel-transported frame, not an up-vector one.** `props.ts`'s `sweep()` carries each ring's reference direction forward by projecting the previous one off the new tangent. Rebuilding it per ring from world up (or from a Frenet normal) flips as the limb passes horizontal and twists the tube inside out — precisely at the branch reaching out of the crown, which is the one you are looking at.
- **A foliage card reads as a green plane unless its texture has holes in it.** `makeLeafTexture` scattered 240 ellipses into a disc they covered twice over, so the cluster was ragged at the rim and solid everywhere else — invisible on a small card, and on a big one exactly the flat slab you see hanging off a crown. It is painted, punched open with `destination-out`, then sown with more leaves over the gaps. The companion rule is size: a card past about half its lobe's radius is wider than the lobe and cannot read as anything but a quad. What is *not* fixable this way is the cards that end up edge-on at the crown's silhouette — for any orientation distribution the same fraction contains the view ray, so widening the facing jitter relocates the slivers instead of removing them.
- **Foliage cards are fill cost; trunk soup is not.** A crown's card count is paid over the whole silhouette of a forest, while a trunk is opaque and a few pixels wide. When `crown()` lobed every fork as well as every tip it doubled a broadleaf's cards for a change nobody could see, so the rule when retuning a tree is: card count stays at or below where it was, vertices in `wood()` may grow. Relatedly, a canopy of perfectly horizontal cards (`cards(..., flat)`) has no thickness in silhouette at all — that is what the `tilt` argument is for, and without it the acacia was a few green streaks floating over the savanna.
- **A root flare must stay inside the kit's `solid.r`** or the player clips into it: collision is one cylinder and the flare is the widest part of the tree. Check `r * (1 + flare * (1 - t/0.22)^1.8)` at the station where the spine crosses y = 0, not at the spine's own origin — `wood()` sinks the trunk below the ground on purpose so the swell is buried.
- **A dome shaded from its own vertex position needs real tessellation.** The cloud shell computes noise from `vDir = position`, which the rasterizer interpolates *linearly* — across a coarse triangle that is a chord, not an arc, so every cloud cell is sheared to the facet it lands on. On the old 20x12 sphere the artifact was invisible only because the noise was low-frequency enough to hide it; raising the frequency drew straight-edged rectangles of cloud across the sky. It is 64x40 now, which is five thousand triangles on a mesh that never moves and does not touch the fill cost the shader is actually made of.
- **Surfaces out here are computed, not mapped.** Chunk geometry is one merged soup with no usable UVs, so brick, shingles, paving and bark come from `world/surface.ts`, which reads world position and the face normal in the fragment shader off a one-float `aSurf` code per stamp. `applyFixedSurface()` does the same for a whole material (the authored house's walls). Wind and surface share a single `onBeforeCompile` because a material only gets one.
- **Ground colour and vegetation must read the same "how paved is this" number** (`settlements.ts`'s `pavedAt`). When they disagreed, every street in town rendered as bare concrete with bright green tufts standing in it.
